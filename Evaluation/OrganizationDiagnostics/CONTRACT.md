# Continuing-project River contract v1

This is a new diagnostic interpretation, not a replacement for frozen P1/P2 labels.
The user approved a continuing-project River: design, execution, results and revisions
of one project belong together. Topic similarity alone is not project identity.

## Pair judgments

- `same`: evidence supports at least one shared continuing project, identifiable by
  its particular goal, undertaking, object/deliverable, client or event. Planning,
  paperwork, implementation, shipping and reviewing that same undertaking can all
  belong together. Different subtasks do not by themselves justify separate Rivers.
- `related`: evidence supports a connection but distinct continuing projects. Shared
  tools, vocabulary, people, suppliers or a reusable template do not establish one
  project. Separately commissioned jobs, independent experiments and different
  events remain separate unless the evidence explicitly establishes one undertaking.
- `unrelated`: sufficient evidence describes different projects with no meaningful
  supported connection. Do not infer a hidden link just because a link is possible.
- `uncertain`: the available text cannot support a reliable choice. Ambiguous
  referents, missing attachments and conflicting project identity warrant abstention.
  A likely guess is not enough for an automatic attachment.

Examples: permission and crate design for one specified museum loan are normally
one continuing project. Two separate loans of different objects are not one River
just because the same courier collects both. Two experiments on the same sensor
are separate when they have independently stated goals and outcomes. An explicit
combined project may contain several experiments; follow the stated scope, not an
invented umbrella such as 'all my work'. A correction of a dimension stays in its
project. A recurring annual event is a separate project per edition unless the
capture explicitly concerns a shared continuing programme.

## Bridges, context and causality

A source can explicitly address two projects and be linked to both without merging
those projects. Pairwise sharing is therefore not transitive. Pair-only judgments
must use only the two visible captures. Context judgments may use the supplied
library, but must cite the evidence that resolves identity; no old memberships,
predictions, scores or reviewer consensus may be used. Full-library review is a
retrospective diagnostic and is not evidence of what an online system knew earlier.

Chronological story evaluation uses only the observed prefix. Future clarifications
cannot retroactively justify a confident earlier assignment. An unresolved capture
has no gold automatic membership until an explicit correction resolves it. Revising
text does not implicitly move membership; explicit corrections change membership.
Archive hides a capture without deleting its content, membership or revision history;
restore makes it active again. A bridge alone never authorizes an automatic merge.

## Review method and limits

Independent agents first save pair judgments, then separately review source-only
context. Each judgment records relation, short evidence-based rationale, and whether
the pair itself is sufficient. Preserve first judgments and disagreements. Root
adjudication explains any final choice; it must not maximize model performance.
Agents share model-family limitations; this is not human validation or ground truth
about a real user's preferences. Report uncertain cases rather than forcing certainty.

No training, threshold tuning, production changes or model qualification is allowed
in checkpoint 1. Samples are deliberately error-enriched and cannot estimate overall
accuracy or prevalence. Old evaluation is now exposed diagnostic material, not a new
held-out test. Retrieval and ledger failures cannot be established by pair review alone.
